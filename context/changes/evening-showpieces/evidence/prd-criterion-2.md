# PRD Primary criterion 2 re-run (M-4 S-05, evening-showpieces)

Criterion (PRD Success Criteria, Primary): "The top-5 ranking for a sampled site and date survives a cross-check against an independent planetarium reference (ephemeris) and an independent observation planner (ranking sanity) on 2-3 nights."

- **Run:** 2026-10-16, after Phases 1-3 of `evening-showpieces`.
- **Nights:** Warsaw 2026-04-15, 2026-07-15 and 2026-10-15 (three of the four calibration nights; April is the counter-case, July and October are the evening nights). Bortle 5, telescope 150/750, minimum altitude 15°, the full Messier + Caldwell catalogue.
- **Final top 5 of each night (engine):**
  - 2026-04-15: M3, M13, M92, M81, M44
  - 2026-07-15: M39, M13, NGC7000, M31, M71
  - 2026-10-15: M31, NGC869, M34, M45, M39

## Leg 1: independent ephemeris (committed, automated)

Skyfield + JPL DE421, generated by `scripts/deep-sky-reference.py` into `src/lib/engine/fixtures/skyfield/deep-sky-warsaw-2026.json` for all 171 catalogue objects on the three nights (the Sun's geometric altitude below -15° for the dark window, refracted altitude at or above 15° for each object's window, one-minute grid). The test `src/lib/engine/window-reference.test.ts` compares the window the app shows (`score.window`) for every object, and for each night's final top 5, with the fixture at the PRD's tolerances (5 minutes, 1°).

Result: **survives.** For the top 5 the largest window-edge difference is 0.02 min (April), 0.01 min (July) and 0.04 min (October), and the largest peak-altitude difference is 0.005°, 0.005° and 0.043°. Before this change the grid edges were up to 9.9 minutes inside the truth and the reference test was red (implementation-notes.md, Phase 1).

## Leg 2: independent observation planner (manual cross-check, nothing committed)

**Planner:** in-the-sky.org (Dominic Ford), the "What's up" object pages for Warsaw, one page per object and night: `https://in-the-sky.org/data/object.php?id=<id>&town=756135&year=<year>&month=<month>&day=<day>`. Credit: data and statements on in-the-sky.org are the work of Dominic Ford; the site's terms allow redistribution with the copyright text and credit but not for profit, so, by the owner's decision of 2026-10-16, no text and no value from the site is stored in this repository. This record holds only the method, the rules and an agreement summary.

**Method.** The pages were read once during the research phase on 2026-10-16 (56 object pages plus two others, a probe page and the terms page: 58 fetches, about 68 HTTP attempts counting retries, spaced at least 2.5 s apart, a User-Agent naming the repository URL, no personal data) for the objects the probes expected in the top 5s; the raw capture is held outside the repository by the lead agent. The final top 5s above were compared with it by hand.

**Rules, fixed before the comparison so the engine could not shape them (plan Phase 4):**

- P1: every top-5 object is shown by the planner as visible that night.
- P2: the planner's highest altitude is at least 30° where the page states one.
- P3: the engine's peak altitude is within 3° of the planner's highest altitude where the page states one and its time lies inside the engine's window.
- A page that states no highest altitude is reported as "not stated" and neither passes nor fails P2 or P3.

**Result: survives, within what the pages say.**

- P1: **15 of 15** top-5 objects are shown as visible on their night.
- P2 and P3: the pages state a highest altitude for **7 of 15** pairs (April 2 of 5, July 2 of 5, October 3 of 5); the other 8 are "not stated" (April 3, July 3, October 2).
  - P2 passes for all 7 stated (all well above 30°).
  - P3 applies to 6 of the 7. The engine's peak altitude agrees with the planner's within 1.3°, and the peak times agree within 6 minutes, for M3 and M13 in April, M71 in July, and NGC869, M45 and M39 in October.
  - For M39 in July the planner's highest point falls at its own dawn limit, after the engine's window ends (the planner's twilight rule is undocumented and differs from the engine's Bortle-5 dark window), so P3 does not apply and is not counted as a pass or a failure.
- Caveats observed about the planner: its twilight rule is undocumented and varies slightly by object, its sentences vary in shape, and a few objects give no numbers. It gives no ranking.

**Planner note.** The planner gives per-object altitude, times and visibility and no ranking, so the ranking is checked against the published beginner list (each entry has at least two sources), not against a planner's ranked list. The PRD names no tool for the planner.
